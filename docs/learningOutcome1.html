Learning Outcome 1: Interactive Media Products

Example 1: Creating an Interactive Prototype for My Portfolio

Action

Before starting on the prototype, I did extensive research on different portfolio websites. During this research, I found Gary Le Masson's portfolio, which creatively used Google's search page as the foundation for his design.
This inspired me to do something similar with YouTube's interface, aiming to showcase my projects and learning outcomes in a familiar but creative way. The idea was to include video clips demonstrating my work, such as group projects or Illustrator creations.
I started by wireframing the layout and created a prototype in Figma. This prototype included a main page, a profile page with contact details, a learning outcomes page, and dedicated pages for each learning outcome.
My initial feedback from peers and teachers was positive. I was encouraged to begin coding the website. I moved ahead with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, implementing a YouTube-like design with a clean layout and interactive elements.

Result

The result of this first iteration was a functioning website with a familiar, user-friendly interface. It was easy to navigate, and the video content added a dynamic element. I completed several learning outcome pages and got some small JavaScript animations working, bringing the site closer to an interactive, engaging portfolio experience. I felt confident with the direction I was heading based on the positive feedback I initially received.

Feedback

The initial feedback from my peers and teachers was quite encouraging. They liked the idea of combining video elements with documentation and thought that using YouTube's layout made the portfolio approachable and visually familiar. They also pointed out that my coding and layout were well-structured. However, when I showcased the portfolio to Stan, a teacher who specializes in JavaScript, he offered a more critical perspective. While he acknowledged the good execution and clean code, he raised a concern: YouTube’s interface is designed for mass video consumption and content streaming, not for showcasing personal work. He suggested that this design might be too overused and not suited to the purpose of a portfolio, which should focus on presenting my skills and creativity. He encouraged me to think outside the box and push for something more unique and impactful, given my capabilities.

Reflection

Stan’s feedback made me reconsider my entire approach. I realized that while the YouTube interface is recognizable and easy to use, it didn’t allow me to fully express my creative potential or make my portfolio stand out. Reflecting on this, I understood how important it is to align the design of a portfolio with its purpose — which, in my case, was to showcase my originality and technical skills. This feedback helped me realize that it’s not just about following trends but about creating something that truly reflects my personal strengths and the uniqueness of my work.

Example 2: Designing and prototyping for CZ.nl website

Action

After conducting thorough research on the existing CZ.nl website, I focused on understanding the key areas for improvement by using various research methods and strategies. Here’s a summary of my research approach:
Individual Research Questions and Strategies My team and I split the analysis of three competitor websites among small groups. Each group consisted of two members who focused on one specific website, and we recorded observations on elements that required improvement. Heuristic Analysis and User Testing To identify usability issues and ensure a user-centered approach, I applied several UX principles, including:
Miller’s Law: Helping users process information without feeling overwhelmed by breaking content into manageable sections.
Jakob’s Law: Ensuring that the site’s functionality aligns with users' expectations based on their experiences with other websites.
Fitts’s Law: Optimizing the size and placement of interactive elements to improve accessibility and ease of navigation. Design Focus Areas


Based on our analysis, we identified the following areas for redesign: Language Toggle: Ensuring the website is accessible in multiple languages. Header & Footer Redesign: Improving layout and information architecture for consistency and usability. Content Redesign: Making the content clearer and more organized for the users. Streamlined Header: Simplifying the navigation to reduce cognitive load. Accessibility: Enhancing accessibility for users with disabilities, such as adding alt text and optimizing color contrasts. After completing this research phase, I started reworking the website prototype in Figma. Using Figma allowed me to have more control over the design elements and make changes more efficiently. I began implementing improvements like the language setting, adjusting header font sizes, and modifying other design aspects to increase readability and functionality. I was also responsible for designing the mobile version of the website, which required additional research on mobile design standards and best practices.

Result

The redesigned prototype demonstrates a significant improvement in usability and visual appeal over the original CZ.nl website. The language toggle, streamlined header, and improved accessibility features contribute to a more user-friendly experience. The mobile version, designed from the ground up, provides a seamless user experience across devices, aligning with the project's accessibility and responsiveness goals.

Feedback

Upon presenting our design to stakeholders, we received feedback highlighting specific areas for further enhancement: Sticky Basket Options: We were advised to explore various design options for the sticky basket to improve user interaction and make it visually appealing. Premium Calculation Selection: Reviewers suggested enhancing the clarity of the steps users must follow to select options for premium calculations. Research Approach and Documentation: During a group discussion, we explained our research steps. The feedback acknowledged the thorough research process and encouraged us to document our research approach further using ICT research methods. Additionally, we were advised to create a requirements list to clarify which elements we wanted to change versus retain from the original design. Source Acknowledgment: We were reminded to properly credit all sources in APA style.

Reflection

Reflecting on the feedback, I realized that while our design effectively improved usability and accessibility, there were still areas to enhance clarity and documentation. The feedback emphasized the importance of thorough documentation and planning, both of which are essential to guiding future design decisions. Our research methodology and adherence to UX principles were well-received, but I see the need to refine the mobile version based on further user testing and research to ensure a cohesive experience across devices.
Additionally, I learned the value of detailing our design requirements from the start, as this would have helped streamline our decision-making process and potentially highlighted areas for improvement earlier.

Decide

Moving forward, I plan to conduct a more in-depth analysis of the mobile design for CZ.nl, incorporating additional research specifically tailored to mobile user behaviors and constraints. This will ensure that our prototype is optimized for mobile users and that we consider all necessary adjustments for smaller screens. For the development phase, I’ll make it a priority to ensure our website has a highly functional, well-designed mobile version. I also plan to apply the feedback regarding documentation by creating a requirements list for the development team, specifying which elements to retain or redesign in the final version. By keeping detailed records and prioritizing mobile optimization, I’m confident that we’ll deliver a product that meets high standards for usability and accessibility.
